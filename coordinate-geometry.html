Stage 1 · Cartesian Physics & Distance

Mapping Space with the Distance Formula

Every location in a 2D plane has a unique coordinate $(x, y)$. By combining horizontal and vertical displacements into a right-angled triangle, Pythagoras' theorem gives us the absolute distance!

Distance in 2D Space & Pythagoras

To find the distance between any two points $A(x_1, y_1)$ and $B(x_2, y_2)$, we form a right triangle with legs $\Delta x = |x_2 - x_1|$ and $\Delta y = |y_2 - y_1|$.

Distance Formula: d = √[ (x₂ - x₁)² + (y₂ - y₁)² ]
Δx = horizontal change, Δy = vertical change
  • Quadrant I (+, +): Both X and Y coordinates are positive.
  • Quadrant II (-, +): X is negative, Y is positive.
  • Quadrant III (-, -): Both X and Y coordinates are negative.
  • Quadrant IV (+, -): X is positive, Y is negative.
Solved Exercise 1.1 — Distance Between Points
Problem: Find distance between $A(-3, 2)$ and $B(3, -4)$.
Solution:
$\Delta x = 3 - (-3) = 6$, $\Delta y = -4 - 2 = -6$
$d = \sqrt{6^2 + (-6)^2} = \sqrt{36 + 36} = \sqrt{72} \approx \mathbf{8.49\text{ units}}$
Result: The hypotenuse length connecting A and B is ~8.49!
🦉

Owl's Lab Tip: "Notice how squaring $\Delta x$ and $\Delta y$ eliminates all negative signs — distance is always positive!"

Weighted Ratio Averages

The Section Formula computes the coordinates of a dividing point $P(x, y)$ by weighting $B$'s coordinates by $m$ and $A$'s coordinates by $n$.

Internal Division: P(x, y) = [ (m·x₂ + n·x₁) / (m+n), (m·y₂ + n·y₁) / (m+n) ]
For Midpoint (m = n = 1): M = [ (x₁ + x₂)/2, (y₁ + y₂)/2 ]
  • Internal Division: Point $P$ lies strictly between $A$ and $B$ on the segment.
  • Midpoint Special Case: When $m : n = 1 : 1$, $P$ is the exact geometric center.
  • External Division: When $P$ lies outside segment $AB$, the formula replaces $+n$ with $-n$.
Solved Exercise 2.1 — 2:3 Internal Division
Problem: Find point $P$ dividing $A(-4, -3)$ and $B(6, 5)$ in ratio $2 : 3$.
Solution:
$x = \frac{2(6) + 3(-4)}{2+3} = \frac{12 - 12}{5} = \mathbf{0}$
$y = \frac{2(5) + 3(-3)}{2+3} = \frac{10 - 9}{5} = \mathbf{0.2}$
Result: Point P is located at (0, 0.2)!
👧

Medhā's Observation: "It works just like a seesaw balance — the point stays closer to whichever end has the larger ratio weight!"

Stage 3 · Line Kinetics & Inclination

Steepness, Slopes & Linear Equations

The slope $m = \tan\theta$ defines a straight line's direction and steepness. Understanding parallel ($m_1 = m_2$) and perpendicular ($m_1 \cdot m_2 = -1$) conditions unlocks linear relationships!

Slope & Line Forms

Slope measures the rate of vertical rise over horizontal run ($m = \Delta y / \Delta x = \tan\theta$, where $\theta$ is the angle with the positive X-axis).

Slope-Intercept Form: y = m·x + c
Parallel Lines: m₁ = m₂ | Perpendicular Lines: m₁ · m₂ = -1
  • Positive Slope ($m > 0$): Line rises from left to right ($\theta < 90^\circ$).
  • Negative Slope ($m < 0$): Line falls from left to right ($\theta > 90^\circ$).
  • Zero Slope ($m = 0$): Horizontal line parallel to X-axis.
  • Undefined Slope ($m \to \infty$): Vertical line parallel to Y-axis.
Solved Exercise 3.1 — Perpendicular Line Slope
Problem: If Line L1 has slope $m_1 = 2$, find the slope of perpendicular Line L2.
Solution:
$m_1 \cdot m_2 = -1 \implies 2 \cdot m_2 = -1 \implies m_2 = \mathbf{-\frac{1}{2} = -0.5}$
Result: Perpendicular slopes are negative reciprocals of each other!
🦉

Owl's Wisdom: "Slope is the secret key used by civil engineers to build safe roads, roofs, and mountain ramps!"

The Shoelace Matrix Formula

Also known as Gauss's area formula, the Shoelace algorithm uses cross-multiplication of vertex coordinates to evaluate polygon area.

Area = ½ | x₁(y₂ - y₃) + x₂(y₃ - y₁) + x₃(y₁ - y₂) |
Collinear Condition: Area = 0 (Points lie on a single line)
  • Determinant Form: Equivalent to half the determinant of a $3\times3$ coordinate matrix.
  • Collinearity Test: If three points yield $\text{Area} = 0$, they are strictly collinear!
  • Clockwise vs Counter-Clockwise: Absolute value signs ensure positive area regardless of vertex ordering.
Solved Exercise 4.1 — Triangle Area Calculation
Problem: Find area of triangle with $A(0,0)$, $B(4,0)$, $C(0,6)$.
Solution:
$\text{Area} = \frac{1}{2} | 0(0 - 6) + 4(6 - 0) + 0(0 - 0) | = \frac{1}{2} | 24 | = \mathbf{12.0\text{ sq. units}}$
Result: The enclosed right triangle covers exactly 12 square units!
👧

Stage 5 · Transformations & Multi-Factor Explorer

Coordinate Transformations & Locus

Shapes in 2D space shift, rotate, and scale through geometric transformations. Understanding how parameters alter coordinates reveals the locus path of moving points!

Translation, Rotation & Locus

A locus is a set of points satisfying a specific geometric condition (e.g. all points at distance $r$ from origin form a circle $x^2 + y^2 = r^2$).

Translation: (x', y') = (x + h, y + k)
Rotation by θ: x' = x·cosθ - y·sinθ, y' = x·sinθ + y·cosθ
  • Translation Shift $(h, k)$: Slides every coordinate horizontally by $h$ and vertically by $k$.
  • Rotation $(\theta)$: Spins coordinates counter-clockwise around origin $(0,0)$.
  • Uniform Scaling $(S)$: Multiplies coordinates by scale factor $S$.
Solved Exercise 5.1 — 90° Rotation of Point
Problem: Rotate point $P(3, 2)$ by $\theta = 90^\circ$ around origin.
Solution:
$x' = 3\cos(90^\circ) - 2\sin(90^\circ) = 3(0) - 2(1) = \mathbf{-2}$
$y' = 3\sin(90^\circ) + 2\cos(90^\circ) = 3(1) + 2(0) = \mathbf{3}$
Result: Point P(3, 2) rotates to P'(-2, 3)!
🦉

Owl's Diagnostic: "Matrix transformations are the foundation of robotics, allowing arms to position tools with sub-millimeter precision!"

Coordinate Geometry in Science & Technology

From global satellite navigation (GPS) to computer graphics rendering and autonomous robotics path planning.

📡

GPS & Geodesic Navigation

Satellites triangulate position coordinates (latitude, longitude) using 3D distance formulas to guide smartphones and aircraft.

  • Global Positioning System (GPS) trilateration
  • Flight path calculation along spherical coordinates
  • GIS mapping and land surveying tools
  • Emergency 911 location tracking
🎮

Computer Graphics & Game Engines

Game engines convert 3D geometry into 2D screen coordinates using linear transformation matrices and Shoelace area tests.

  • 2D/3D matrix rotation and perspective projection
  • Collision detection using distance formulas
  • Triangle rasterization in GPU graphics hardware
  • UI layout coordinate positioning
🤖

Robotics & Autonomous Vehicles

Self-driving cars and robotic arms use slope vectors and section formulas to navigate obstacle-free trajectories.

  • LIDAR point-cloud coordinate mapping
  • Inverse kinematics for robotic joint control
  • Path planning along perpendicular bisector lines
  • Autonomous drone waypoint navigation
